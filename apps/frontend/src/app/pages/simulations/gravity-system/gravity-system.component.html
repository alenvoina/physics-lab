Gravity Lab – Theory

This simulation visualizes how bodies interact via gravity. Every object with mass attracts every other object with a force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them (Newton's Law of Gravitation):

F = G * (m₁ * m₂) / r²

Modes of the Simulation:

  • Orbit: Bodies move in stable orbits where the gravitational pull is balanced by their tangential velocity. This demonstrates circular and elliptical motion, centripetal force, and energy conservation (kinetic + potential energy remains roughly constant).
  • Galaxy: Many bodies rotate around a central massive body, forming spiral patterns. This shows collective gravitational interactions, angular momentum conservation, and how structures like galaxies form.
  • Chaos: Bodies start with random positions and velocities. Small differences in initial conditions lead to very different trajectories over time, illustrating the concept of chaotic dynamics in N-body gravitational systems.

Energy Visualization:

The graphs show kinetic energy (energy of motion) and potential energy (energy stored in gravitational fields). Total energy helps track how the system conserves energy over time.

Physics Behind the Simulation:

Each body in the system is modeled with:

  • Mass (m) – determines the gravitational pull it exerts.
  • Position and velocity – updated every step using Newton's second law (F = m * a).
  • Gravitational interaction – every pair of bodies exerts forces on each other, summed to find acceleration.

By exploring different modes, you can see real physics in action: from predictable orbits to chaotic interactions, all governed by fundamental laws of motion and gravitation.

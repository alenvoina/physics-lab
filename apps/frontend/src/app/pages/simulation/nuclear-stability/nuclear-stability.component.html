Nuclear Stability

Not all nuclei are created equal. Some configurations of protons and neutrons are stable, others are inherently unstable and decay over time.

Stability depends on a delicate balance. Protons repel each other electrically, while the strong nuclear force binds nucleons together.

Too many or too few neutrons disrupt this balance. The nucleus becomes unstable and transforms into a more stable configuration.

This is why isotopes exist — same element, different neutron count, and completely different stability.

The concept of binding energy explains why this happens: the more tightly bound a nucleus is, the more stable it becomes.

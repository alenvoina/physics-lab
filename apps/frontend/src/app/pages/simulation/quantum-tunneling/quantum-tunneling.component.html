What is Quantum Tunneling?

In classical physics, a particle facing a barrier behaves like a ball hitting a wall: if it doesn’t have enough energy, it simply bounces back.

But in quantum mechanics, particles are not just points — they are waves. And waves don’t stop abruptly. They spread, interfere, and even penetrate barriers.

Inside the barrier, the wave doesn’t vanish — it decays. If the barrier is thin enough, part of the wave emerges on the other side.

This is tunneling — not breaking the barrier, but leaking through it.

Why it matters

  • Electrons move through materials
  • Nuclear fusion happens in stars
  • Modern electronics rely on it

Radioactive Decay

Radioactive decay is a random process in which unstable atomic nuclei lose energy by emitting radiation. Unlike classical processes, it is impossible to predict exactly when a single atom will decay.

Types of Decay

  • Alpha: emission of a helium nucleus (2 protons + 2 neutrons)
  • Beta: transformation of a neutron into a proton (or vice versa)
  • Gamma: emission of high-energy photons

Decay Rate

The decay rate depends on a constant probability per unit time. Even though each atom behaves randomly, large groups follow precise statistical laws.

Half-life

Half-life is the time required for half of the atoms in a sample to decay. This value is constant for a given substance and does not depend on initial quantity.

Applications

  • Radiocarbon dating
  • Nuclear energy production
  • Medical imaging and cancer treatment
